# Surface (Lining) Stratified Squamous Epithelial Cell of the Human Palatine Tonsil

Atlas: An atlas of cells in the human tonsil (Massoni-Badosa et al., 2024) — Azimuth human_tonsil_v2 reference (DOI: 10.1016/j.immuni.2024.01.006)
Scope: adult
Tissue context: palatine tonsil surface (lining) epithelium
Cell Ontology: [keratinocyte](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000312) (CL:0000312, broad match — no exact CL term) — NTR: obophenotype/cell-ontology#3681

## Summary

The tonsil surface ("Outer surface") epithelial cell is a keratinizing, stratified
squamous epithelial cell that forms the outer lining of the human palatine tonsil.
It is one of the two epithelial compartments of the tonsil — the surface (lining)
epithelium and the reticulated crypt epithelium — and is anatomically continuous
with the stratified squamous epithelium of the oral mucosa (Sarmiento Varón et al.,
2021). In the reference atlas, the tonsillar epithelial clusters were annotated as
overlapping the keratinocyte populations of the oral mucosa (Massoni-Badosa et al.,
2024). The surface compartment is distinguished from crypt epithelium by its keratin
profile: the surface epithelium strongly expresses the basal keratins K5 (KRT5) and
K14 (KRT14) at the protein level, whereas the simple keratins K8/K18 and K19 mark the
reticulated crypt (Okda et al., 2021). Its principal function is to form a mechanical
and immunological barrier at the oropharyngeal surface (Sarmiento Varón et al., 2021).

## Markers

The defining markers of the surface compartment are the stratified/basal keratins
**KRT5 (K5)** and **KRT14 (K14)**. In human tonsillar epithelial cells characterised
with a keratin antibody panel, K5 and K14 label the surface stratified epithelium
while K8/K18 and K19 label the crypt (Okda et al., 2021). These marker assignments
are **protein-level (immunostaining)** observations, not transcript measurements:

> "we stained the cells with a panel of monoclonal antibodies against epithelial K5 and K14 as markers of surface stratified epithelial cells (Fig. 3A) and K8/18 and K19 (Fig. 3B) as markers of the tonsillar crypts."
>
> — Okda et al. (2021)

> "K5 and K14 were strongly expressed in the surface epithelium."
>
> — Okda et al. (2021)

> "We found that the differentiated HTECs consisted of a layered surface stratified epithelium with K5 + and K14 + cells (Fig. 3A)."
>
> — Okda et al. (2021)

K5 (KRT5) and K14 (KRT14) form the canonical basal-keratinocyte intermediate-filament
pair, consistent with the surface epithelium being a keratinocyte-type stratified
squamous epithelium. The reciprocal simple/crypt keratins K8, K18 and K19 are the
negative markers that separate crypt from surface — K8 in particular is only weakly
present in the surface compartment (again a protein-level observation):

> "K8 conventionally partners with K18 and was weakly expressed in keratinocytes of the surface tonsil epithelium but was strongly expressed in the reticulated crypts, specifically covering the upper layers of the crypts."
>
> — Okda et al. (2021)

At the transcriptomic level, the reference atlas resolved the tonsillar epithelium
into three clusters overlapping oral-mucosa keratinocytes (one being the FDCSP
epithelium cluster), but did not report K5/K14 transcript values for the surface
cluster specifically in the traversed snippets (Massoni-Badosa et al., 2024):

> "In the epithelial compartment, we identified three clusters overlapping with the keratinocyte populations of the oral mucosa (Figures S6A-S6C)."
>
> — Massoni-Badosa et al. (2024)

> "One of these clusters expressed FDCSP (FDCSP epithelium)."
>
> — Massoni-Badosa et al. (2024)

## Location

The surface epithelium forms the outer periphery of the palatine tonsil and is
continuous with the oral mucosal epithelium. Sarmiento Varón et al. (2021) state that
the surface epithelium is anatomically an extension of the oral mucosa:

> "Anatomically, the surface epithelium of the palatine tonsils is an extension of the stratified squamous epithelium of the oral mucosa (Figure 3A, Supplementary Figures 6, 7)."
>
> — Sarmiento Varón et al. (2021)

It is spatially and structurally distinct from the reticulated crypt epithelium, which
represents deep invaginations where the continuity of the surface epithelium is broken
(Crowell et al., 2025):

> "Tonsil epithelium consist of two different types of actively differentiating epithelium: lining (surface) stratified squamous epithelium; and, reticulated crypts -deep invaginations of the tonsil in which the continuity of the outer surface epithelium is disrupted."
>
> — Crowell et al. (2025)

In the spatial-transcriptomic map of the tonsil, surface epithelium was recovered
predominantly in adult tissue sections, consistent with the adult scope of this
annotation (Crowell et al., 2025):

> "we delineated 45 epithelial substructures comprising both crypt (all sections) and surface epithelium (adult sections only), based on the point pattern density of epithelial cells"
>
> — Crowell et al. (2025)

Independent immunocytochemistry of human palatine tonsil confirms the two-compartment
architecture, identifying cytokeratin-positive stratified squamous (surface) epithelium
alongside reticulated cryptal epithelium (Kumar et al., 2006):

> "Epithelial cells in stratified squamous epithelium and cryptal epithelium were identified with a polyclonal rabbit anti-cytokeratin antibody (Figure 1a, b)."
>
> — Kumar et al. (2006)

## Function

The surface epithelium acts as the first-line mechanical and immunological barrier of
the tonsil, protecting underlying tissue and mediating antigen transport from the
oropharyngeal lumen to the lymphoid compartment (Sarmiento Varón et al., 2021):

> "The tonsillar epithelium provides protection as well as serving to transport foreign material from the lumen to the lymphoid compartment."
>
> — Sarmiento Varón et al. (2021)

Its keratin intermediate-filament content underlies this barrier role, providing
mechanical strength and protection from stress (Crowell et al., 2025):

> "Both surface and crypt epithelial cells contain keratins, a protein family that forms intermediate filaments and maintains cell structure and function, contributing to mechanical strength and protection from stress."
>
> — Crowell et al. (2025)

## Structure / Morphology

The surface epithelium is a multilayered stratified squamous epithelium forming the
outer periphery of the tonsil, in contrast to the spongy, lymphocyte-infiltrated crypt
epithelium (Jackson et al., 2023):

> "The surface epithelium of tonsils consists primarily of stratified squamous cells and makes up the outer periphery, whereas crypt epithelium serves to increase the overall surface area and is composed of an uneven mixture of stratified squamous cells and non-epithelial cells (e.g., infiltrating lymphocytes), resulting in a spongy appearance."
>
> — Jackson et al. (2023)

A distinguishing morphological feature of the surface compartment is apical
keratinization/cornification, reflecting the terminal squamous differentiation of a
keratinizing epithelium (Jackson et al., 2023):

> "The T3 sample more closely resembles surface epithelium, with more layers of stratified squamous cells and signs of apical keratinization/cornification."
>
> — Jackson et al. (2023)
