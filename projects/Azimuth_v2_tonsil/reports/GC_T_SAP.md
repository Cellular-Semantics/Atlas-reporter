# SAP-Expressing Germinal Center T Cell (GC-Tfh) in Human Tonsil

Atlas: Human Tonsil v2 (King et al., 2021) (DOI: 10.1126/sciimmunol.abh3768)
Scope: adult
Tissue context: palatine tonsil (germinal center)
Cell Ontology: [germinal center T cell](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0009062) (CL:0009062, broad match — no exact CL term) — NTR: obophenotype/cell-ontology#3671

## Summary

The Azimuth human tonsil v2 fine label "SAP" denotes a germinal center T-cell
state named for high expression of SAP (SLAM-associated protein, encoded by
*SH2D1A*). SAP is an intracellular SH2-domain adaptor protein — not a surface
marker — that is a validated protein-level marker of germinal center T cells in
human lymphoid tissue and that functions as a critical switch for T:B cell
communication. Biologically this population corresponds to a SAP-high germinal
center T follicular helper (GC-Tfh) cell: SAP expression in CD4 T cells is
essential for germinal center formation, and SAP is required to sustain stable
adhesion between the helper T cell and its cognate B cell in the germinal
centre. Loss of SAP (as in X-linked lymphoproliferative disease, XLP1) abrogates
germinal centre responses and long-term humoral immunity while leaving T:dendritic
cell interactions intact, underscoring that this cell state is defined by
SAP-dependent T:B help.

## Markers

The defining feature of this population is SAP (SLAM-associated protein, gene
*SH2D1A*). It is important to note that SAP is a **cytoplasmic adaptor protein,
not a cell-surface protein**; consequently its detection is by transcript, by
functional/genetic assays, or by intracellular protein staining (immunohistology)
rather than by surface flow markers. Roncador et al. (2007) established SAP as an
immunohistologic marker of germinal center T cells, explicitly noting its
intracytoplasmic localisation:

> "In the present paper we report that SAP , an intracytoplasmic molecule that is involved in cell signaling, is an immunohistologic marker for germinal center T cells in paraffinembedded tissue."
>
> — Roncador et al. (2007)

At the protein level SAP is co-expressed with the germinal center T-cell surface
marker PD-1, with SAP appearing to be induced slightly ahead of PD-1 as helper T
cells enter the follicle (Roncador et al., 2007):

> "ouble labeling for SAP in combination with PD-1, a known marker of germinal center T cells, confirmed that these two molecules are usually co-expressed in the same cells, although the presence of scattered SAP-positive, PD-1-negative T cells in interfollicular areas suggested that SAP is induced slightly in advance of PD-1, before helper T cells enter the germinal center."
>
> — Roncador et al. (2007)

SAP is biochemically defined as a small SH2-domain adaptor encoded by *SH2D1A*
(Yusuf et al., 2010):

> "SAP (SLAM-associated protein), a small Src homology domain 2-domain adaptor protein encoded by the gene SH2D1A, is integral to T cell-dependent humoral immunity."
>
> — Yusuf et al. (2010)

Its expression is largely restricted to T cells, NK cells, and NKT cells and is
minimal in B cells (Li et al., 2025):

> "SAP is predominantly expressed in T cells, NK cells, and Natural Killer T (NKT) cells, where it modulates SLAM family receptor (SFR)-mediated signaling pathways, but is minimally expressed in B cells (41)."
>
> — Li et al. (2025)

## Location

This cell type resides in the germinal centres of secondary lymphoid tissue,
including the palatine tonsil of the atlas. SAP marks T cells within the germinal
center specifically, and is present in a minor SAP-positive, PD-1-negative
population in interfollicular areas that appears to represent cells just prior to
follicle entry (Roncador et al., 2007, quoted above). Functionally the
SAP-dependent programme operates at the T:B interface within the germinal centre,
where sustained adhesion between the helper T cell and cognate B cell takes place
(Cannons et al., 2010; Li et al., 2025):

> "Specifically, SAP deficiency severely impairs the adhesion of CD4 Tfh cells to B cells in GCs, resulting in compromised humoral immunity and hypogammaglobulinemia."
>
> — Li et al. (2025)

## Function

### Sustaining stable T:B cell interactions

The central function of SAP in this cell type is to enable stable, sustained
contact between the helper T cell and its cognate B cell. Two-photon intravital
imaging showed that SAP is selectively required for T:B — but not T:dendritic
cell — interactions (Qi et al., 2008):

> "Here, using two-photon intravital imaging, we show that SAP deficiency selectively impairs the ability of CD4+ T cells to stably interact with cognate B cells but not antigen-presenting dendritic cells."
>
> — Qi et al. (2008)

This adhesion is a multi-stage process in which an early integrin-dependent phase
is followed by a sustained SAP-dependent phase mediated by SLAM-family receptors
such as CD84 and Ly108 (Cannons et al., 2010):

> "CD4+ T cells deficient in signaling lymphocyte activation molecule (SLAM)-associated protein (SAP) exhibit a selective impairment in adhesion to antigen presenting B cells but not dendritic cells (DC), resulting in defective germinal center formation."
>
> — Cannons et al. (2010)

> "We further found that the SLAM family member, CD84, was required for prolonged T:B cell contact, optimal T follicular helper function, and germinal center formation in vivo."
>
> — Cannons et al. (2010)

Mechanistically, SAP acts as an on-off switch on SLAM-family receptors: in its
absence, receptors such as Ly108 (SLAMF6) transmit inhibitory (SHP-1-dependent)
signals that curtail T:B adhesion (Kageyama et al., 2012):

> "The ability of SAP to regulate both positive and negative signals in T cells can explain the severity of SAP-deficiency and highlights the importance of SAP and SHP-1 competition for Ly108 ITSM binding as a rheostat for the magnitude of T cell help to B cells."
>
> — Kageyama et al. (2012)

SAP binds SLAM-family receptors and shapes adhesion, cytokine secretion, and TCR
signalling strength, thereby controlling the duration of T:B contact (Hu et al.,
2013):

> "SAP binds to SLAM family receptors and mediates signaling that affects cell adhesion, cytokine secretion, and TCR signaling strength."
>
> — Hu et al. (2013)

### Germinal center formation and B-cell help

Because stable T:B contact is required to deliver help, SAP expression in CD4 T
cells is essential for germinal center development and for the GC-Tfh subset
itself (Yusuf et al., 2010; Gartshteyn et al., 2021):

> "Signaling lymphocytic activation molecule (SLAM)-associated protein (SAP [SH2D1A]) expression in CD4 T cells is essential for GC development."
>
> — Yusuf et al. (2010)

> "Strikingly, SAP-deficient mice have an absence of the GC TFH cell subset and SAP− TFH cells are defective in IL-4 and IL-21 production."
>
> — Yusuf et al. (2010)

> "Subsequent work identified an essential requirement for SAP in the development of T FH cells in the germinal center, absence of which is associated with decreased T FH IL-4 production, loss of T FH -B cell help activity, and decreased antibody production (33)(34)(35)."
>
> — Gartshteyn et al. (2021)

The requirement is intrinsic to the T cell — SAP acts in T cells and not in B
cells (Gartshteyn et al., 2021):

> "As mentioned, it is the expression of SAP in T cells, and not in B cells, that is required and sufficient for SAP-dependent antibody production and germinal center formation (37)."
>
> — Gartshteyn et al. (2021)

The failure of stable T:B contact prevents the helper T cell from being retained
in the germinal centre and from sustaining the reaction (Qi et al., 2008):

> "Furthermore, the lack of stable interactions with B cells renders Sap-/- T cells unable to be efficiently recruited to and retained in a nascent germinal centre to sustain the germinal centre reaction."
>
> — Qi et al. (2008)

### Clinical significance

The clinical consequence of losing this function is seen in X-linked
lymphoproliferative disease type 1 (XLP1), caused by loss-of-function mutations
in *SH2D1A* (Qi et al., 2008; Li et al., 2025):

> "In human X-linked lymphoproliferative disease and its gene-targeted mouse model, loss-of-function mutations in signalling lymphocyte activation molecule-associated protein (SAP, encoded by SH2D1a) cause a profound defect in germinal centre formation by an as yet unknown mechanism."
>
> — Qi et al. (2008)

> "while defective SAP-dependent T follicular helper (TFH) cell function compromises germinal centers (GCs) formation and humoral immunity."
>
> — Li et al. (2025)

## Structure / Morphology

No evidence found in traversed literature. The distinguishing feature is molecular
(intracellular SAP/*SH2D1A* expression and SAP-dependent T:B synapse formation)
rather than a described cellular morphology; the SLAM-associated adaptor is a
cytoplasmic signalling molecule, and the relevant "structure" is the sustained
immunological synapse formed with the cognate B cell (Cannons et al., 2010;
Gartshteyn et al., 2021).
