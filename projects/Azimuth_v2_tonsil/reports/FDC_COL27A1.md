# COL27A1+ Follicular Dendritic Cells of the Human Tonsil Germinal Center

Atlas: Azimuth human_tonsil_v2 (King et al., 2021) (DOI: 10.1126/sciimmunol.abh3768)
Scope: adult
Tissue context: palatine tonsil (B-cell follicle / germinal center)
Cell Ontology: [follicular dendritic cell](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000442) (CL:0000442, broad match — no exact CL term) — NTR: obophenotype/cell-ontology#3669

## Summary

The Azimuth human tonsil (v2) fine annotation "COL27A1+" (order 67) labels a
stromal subset of follicular dendritic cells (FDCs) distinguished by expression
of *COL27A1*, the gene encoding the alpha-1 chain of type XXVII collagen. FDCs
are non-hematopoietic, mesenchymally derived reticular cells that reside in the
B-cell follicle and germinal center, where they build a CXCL13-secreting
scaffold, capture and retain antigen as immune complexes, and organize the
germinal-center reaction (Jarjour et al., 2014; Pikor et al., 2020). Single-cell
transcriptomic studies have shown that FDCs are not homogeneous but comprise
phenotypically distinct light-zone (LZ) and dark-zone (DZ) subsets that underpin
the two functional compartments of the germinal center (Pikor et al., 2020;
Lütge et al., 2021; Onder et al., 2021). *COL27A1* is the atlas-assigned
discriminating transcript for this FDC subset; the marker itself is defined here
at the transcript level, and no protein-level (flow/IHC) validation of COL27A1
in tonsil FDCs was found in the traversed literature.

## Markers

The atlas subset is named for *COL27A1*, which encodes an extracellular-matrix
fibrillar-associated collagen (Hu et al., 2022):

> "COL27A1 is responsible for encoding one proalpha chain in type XXVII collagen, which can join 42 additional genes and integrate their protein products for the formation of 28 or more diverse trimeric collagens [24]."
>
> — Hu et al. (2022)

This is consistent with the role of FDCs and related follicular stroma as
extracellular-matrix–producing mesenchymal cells. In this atlas, COL27A1 is a
transcript-level discriminating marker; canonical FDC identity markers reported
at protein/receptor level are the complement and Fc receptors. Anania et al.
(2021) note that these receptors define the light-zone FDC phenotype:

> "The fore mentioned LZ FDCs express FcRs and CRs and can be characterized by high expression of CXCL13."
>
> — Anania et al. (2021)

At the transcriptomic level, Lütge et al. (2021) describe the same canonical
markers and their differential use across FDC subsets:

> "Classically, FDCs are identified based on the expression of complement receptor 2 (Cr2) and Fc receptors (ie, Fcgr2b and Fcer2a)."
>
> — Lütge et al. (2021)

> "LZ-FDCs express significantly higher levels of Cr2 and Fcer2a and interact with B cells and follicular helper T cells in the light zone during the germinal center reaction."
>
> — Lütge et al. (2021)

## Location

FDCs constitute the stromal compartment of the B-cell follicle and germinal
center. Rodda et al. (2015) place the germinal-center subdivision and the FDC
niche precisely:

> "The germinal center (GC) is divided into a dark zone (DZ) and a light zone (LZ)."
>
> — Rodda et al. (2015)

> "Follicular dendritic cells (FDCs) are well characterized for their role in supporting B cell Ag encounter in primary follicles and in the GC LZ."
>
> — Rodda et al. (2015)

The FDC network is remodelled with the germinal-center reaction, and distinct
subsets partition between the two zones. Pikor et al. (2020) describe this
reorganization:

> "Upon inflammation, the centrally distributed FDC network of the primary B cell follicle is reorganized to underpin the GC light zone (LZ), and a yet unknown reticular cell type is thought to mature to support the emerging GC dark zone (DZ)."
>
> — Pikor et al. (2020)

Onder et al. (2021) summarize the resulting zonal FDC heterogeneity, the
biological setting into which a COL27A1+ FDC subset falls:

> "The germinal center reaction triggers the reorganization of the B cell follicle into light zone (LZ) and dark zone (DZ) areas underpinned by phenotypically distinct FDC subsets."
>
> — Onder et al. (2021)

> "Transcriptomic analysis of CXCL13expressing BRCs further showed that LZ-and DZ-FDC subsets are present in the primary follicle and are poised to generate the respective microenvironments that steer the germinal center reaction through CXCL12-mediated positioning of follicular helper T cells and B cells"
>
> — Onder et al. (2021)

## Function

FDCs organize the humoral immune response by nucleating and maintaining the
germinal center. Jarjour et al. (2014) describe their scaffolding and
chemokine-secreting roles:

> "They secrete the B cell follicle homing chemokine CXCL13 and constitute a cellular scaffold for B cell migration"
>
> — Jarjour et al. (2014)

> "During immune responses, FDCs act as antigen-presenting and -retaining cells that remodel the primary follicular network into germinal centers (GCs), a specialized structure in which B cells proliferate, undergo somatic hypermutation, and carry out class switching"
>
> — Jarjour et al. (2014)

Antigen capture and presentation are the defining functional specialisation of
FDCs, and CXCL13 secretion is their most characteristic feature (Pikor et al.,
2020):

> "Follicular dendritic cells (FDCs) specialize in the capture and presentation of antigen, and are the reticular cell type most commonly associated with CXCL13 expression."
>
> — Pikor et al. (2020)

The functional division of labour between FDC subsets maps onto germinal-center
zones: LZ-FDCs support antigen presentation to and selection of germinal-center
B cells and follicular helper T cells (Lütge et al., 2021; Anania et al., 2021),
while distinct dark-zone stroma supports proliferating B cells (Rodda et al.,
2015).

## Structure / Morphology

FDCs are large stromal cells with an extended dendritic-like morphology, and
despite their name are not related to hematopoietic dendritic cells. Anania et
al. (2021) note their identity and origin:

> "In spite of their name, they are not related to conventional dendritic cells, do not express MHC-II, and consequently do not present peptides to T cells."
>
> — Anania et al. (2021)

> "FDCs are not derived from hematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow but from ubiquitous mural perivascular precursors"
>
> — Anania et al. (2021)

Their reticular, process-bearing morphology was described in classical studies
and confirmed by fate mapping (Jarjour et al., 2014):

> "FDCs were characterized decades ago as large follicle-associated dendritic-like cells displaying multiple long centrifugal processes in constant interaction with B cells"
>
> — Jarjour et al. (2014)

FDCs can be resolved into zonal subsets, into which the atlas COL27A1+ subset
falls (Anania et al., 2021):

> "FDCs can be divided into two subsets, LZ and dark zone (DZ) FDCs"
>
> — Anania et al. (2021)
