# Crypt (Reticulated) Epithelial Cell of the Human Palatine Tonsil

Atlas: An atlas of cells in the human tonsil (Massoni-Badosa et al., 2024) — Azimuth human_tonsil_v2 reference (DOI: 10.1016/j.immuni.2024.01.006)
Scope: adult
Tissue context: palatine tonsil crypt (reticulated) epithelium
Cell Ontology: [M cell of gut](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000682) (CL:0000682, narrow match — existing term is narrower) — NTR: obophenotype/cell-ontology#3682

## Summary

The "Crypt" epithelial cell of the Azimuth human_tonsil_v2 reference (Massoni-Badosa et al., 2024) corresponds to the epithelium lining the deep invaginations (crypts) of the human palatine tonsil. Anatomically, this compartment is the reticulated crypt epithelium, also called lymphoepithelium — a specialized, antigen-sampling epithelium that is heavily interspersed with lymphocytes and other immune cells, in contrast to the ordered stratified squamous epithelium of the tonsillar surface (Arambula et al., 2021; Crowell et al., 2025). It is a functional immunological compartment: its epithelial layer is thin, its basement membrane is discontinuous or interrupted where lymphocytes infiltrate, and it harbours microfold (M) cells that transport luminal antigen to underlying lymphoid tissue (Arambula et al., 2021; Raju et al., 2012; Kanaya et al., 2012). As a simple/reticulated epithelium it expresses simple-epithelial keratins (K8/K18/K19; KRT8/KRT18/KRT19), while its M-cell contingent is defined transcriptionally by the Ets transcription factor SPIB and the early M-cell marker MARCKSL1 (Brembeck & Rustgi, 2000; Kanaya et al., 2012). The atlas itself resolves the tonsil epithelial compartment into three keratinocyte-overlapping clusters, one of which (FDCSP epithelium) had been described in leukocyte-infiltrated crypts (Massoni-Badosa et al., 2024).

## Markers

The retrieved atlas snippets characterise the tonsillar epithelial compartment broadly rather than enumerating crypt-epithelial keratins; marker assignment at the transcript level is available through the atlas data resource (HCATonsilData/Azimuth), and the marker biology below is grounded in the supporting literature.

**Simple-epithelial keratins — KRT8 / KRT18 / KRT19 (protein and transcript level).** Reticulated crypt epithelium is a simple/non-keratinizing epithelium, and simple epithelia are defined by the K8/K18 keratin pair, with K19 as a closely associated partner:

> "Simple epithelia express K8 and K18 (4)."
>
> — Brembeck & Rustgi (2000)

Keratin 19 (KRT19) is the smallest acidic keratin and behaves as a flexible partner that marks simple and proliferative epithelia:

> "The most striking exception to the keratin-pair rule is the smallest known acidic keratin, namely keratin 19 (K19). K19 has no known basic type II keratin partner, although its expression is often found in cells which express K8"
>
> — Brembeck & Rustgi (2000)

> "in some tissues, K19 can substitute for K18, the usual partner of K8"
>
> — Brembeck & Rustgi (2000)

The human-keratin reference framework confirms K8 as the usual type II partner in simple epithelia (Moll et al., 2008):

> "For pair formation, its type II partner usually appears to be K8."
>
> — Moll et al. (2008)

**M-cell markers — SPIB and MARCKSL1 (transcript/protein level).** The antigen-transporting M-cell contingent of reticulated/follicle-associated epithelium is defined by the Ets transcription factor Spi-B (SPIB) and the early precursor marker MARCKSL1 (Kanaya et al., 2012):

> "Based on these observations, Spi-B has a crucial role in the differentiation of morphologically and functionally mature M cells, and its role in cellular differentiation is specific to M cells among the known types of intestinal epithelial cells."
>
> — Kanaya et al. (2012)

> "Thus, the residual Marcksl1 + Annexin V + cells in Spib −/− mice are defective in their ability to take up luminal antigens."
>
> — Kanaya et al. (2012)

MARCKSL1 marks early M-cell precursors, which upon SPIB induction go on to express mature M-cell markers such as CCL9 and GP2 (Kanaya et al., 2012):

> "the remaining Marcksl1 + Annexin V + cells start coexpressing subsequent M-cell markers, such as CCL9 and GP2. Considering that Spi-B is essential for the expression of CCL9, GP2, M-Sec and Sgne-1 in M cells"
>
> — Kanaya et al. (2012)

## Location

### In the palatine tonsil

The crypt epithelial cell lines the branching tonsillar crypts. Developmentally, the crypts arise as epithelial invaginations of the second branchial pouch (Arambula et al., 2021):

> "The medial epithelial surface of the tonsil forms from the second branchial pouch, as solid epithelial cores invaginate into the surrounding mesenchyme. These cores eventually canalize and form crypts."
>
> — Arambula et al. (2021)

The crypt lining is a mixture of ordinary stratified squamous epithelium and the specialized reticulated crypt epithelium (lymphoepithelium), which is directly analogous to the follicle-associated epithelium of gut Peyer's patches (Arambula et al., 2021):

> "The crypts themselves are lined by a non-uniform distribution of stratified squamous epithelium and reticulated crypt epithelium. This latter epithelium, also referred to as lymphoepithelium, is similar to Peyer's patches found in the gastrointestinal tract."
>
> — Arambula et al. (2021)

Spatial transcriptomic mapping of the human tonsil places the reticulated crypts as deep invaginations where the surface epithelium is disrupted, distinct from the lining surface epithelium (Crowell et al., 2025):

> "Tonsil epithelium consist of two different types of actively differentiating epithelium: lining (surface) stratified squamous epithelium; and, reticulated crypts -deep invaginations of the tonsil in which the continuity of the outer surface epithelium is disrupted."
>
> — Crowell et al. (2025)

The atlas confirms that the reticulated crypt lining reported by histology corresponds to a transcriptomically distinct epithelial subpopulation. It resolved the epithelial compartment into three keratinocyte-overlapping clusters, one of which had previously been described specifically in crypts (Massoni-Badosa et al., 2024):

> "In the epithelial compartment, we identified three clusters overlapping with the keratinocyte populations of the oral mucosa (Figures S6A-S6C)."
>
> — Massoni-Badosa et al. (2024)

> "One of these clusters expressed FDCSP (FDCSP epithelium). FDCSP was first described in FDC and in ''leukocyte-infiltrated tonsillar crypts,'' although the specific population within the crypts remained unknown."
>
> — Massoni-Badosa et al. (2024)

> "Here, we provide evidence that FDSCP-expressing cells represent a specific subpopulation of the tonsillar epithelium."
>
> — Massoni-Badosa et al. (2024)

The subepithelial region directly beneath the crypt lining is a preferential niche for tissue-resident immune cells, underscoring the tight epithelial–immune apposition at this site (Massoni-Badosa et al., 2024):

> "Using multiplexed immunofluorescence histology, we identified tonsillar RM CD8 T within the epithelium and in the subepithelial connective tissue septum lining the tonsillar crypts, a preferential localization site also for other tissueresident immune populations, such as long-lived PC and ILC"
>
> — Massoni-Badosa et al. (2024)

## Function

The palatine tonsil sits at the entrance of the aerodigestive tract and is under constant antigenic exposure (Massoni-Badosa et al., 2024):

> "Palatine tonsils are under constant exposure to antigens via the upper respiratory tract, which makes them a compelling model secondary lymphoid organ (SLO)"
>
> — Massoni-Badosa et al. (2024)

The defining function of the crypt (reticulated) epithelial cell is antigen sampling and transport. Its thin, lymphocyte-permeated architecture allows luminal antigens to be delivered directly to underlying immune cells (Arambula et al., 2021):

> "With lymphocytes and dendritic cells just deep to the epithelial surface, this histologic arrangement allows for rapid transport and presentation of exogenous antigens to the lymphoid cells for efficient initiation of an immune response."
>
> — Arambula et al. (2021)

The reticulated crypt epithelium is treated as a functional immune compartment with a defined set of roles, including facilitating immune-cell/antigen contact and direct antigen transport (Tabou et al., 2020):

> "The reticulated crypt epithelium and surface epithelium regarded as functional compartments in the palatine tonsils where the immune reactions occur."
>
> — Tabou et al. (2020)

> "Mal et al., (2008) recommended that the major functions of the reticulated epithelium are to provide a favorable environment for the intimate contact between the effector cells of immune responses, to facilitate direct transport of antigens, to synthesise the secretory component continually and to contain a pool of immunoglobulins."
>
> — Tabou et al. (2020)

The crypts are highlighted as a specialized immunological compartment central to tonsil function (Crowell et al., 2025):

> "The crypts represent a specialized compartment, important in the immunological functions of the tonsil, as they facilitate the interaction between immune cells and antigens, aiding in the recognition and removal of pathogens that enter the body through the oral cavity"
>
> — Crowell et al. (2025)

Antigen transport itself is carried out by M (microfold) cells embedded in this epithelium. In the homologous intestinal follicle-associated epithelium, the transcription factor Spi-B is required for functional, antigen-transcytosing M cells, and its loss abolishes luminal antigen uptake (Kanaya et al., 2012, quoted under Markers). M cells can also be induced from ordinary follicle-associated epithelial cells in response to bacterial colonization, reflecting the antigen-responsive plasticity of this compartment (Chin et al., 2006):

> "Differentiation of epithelial cells to M cells in response to bacterial colonization on the follicle-associated epithelium of Peyer's patch in rat small intestine."
>
> — Chin et al. (2006)

> "Ultrastructural study on the differentiation and the fate of M cells in follicle-associated epithelium of rat Peyer's patch."
>
> — Onishi et al. (2007)

## Structure / Morphology

The reticulated crypt epithelium is structurally distinct from ordered stratified squamous epithelium: it is less orderly, thin, contains both epithelial and non-epithelial (lymphoid) cells, and may lack a basement membrane (Arambula et al., 2021):

> "Reticulated epithelium is less orderly than stratified squamous epithelium and contains both epithelial and non-epithelial cells, particularly lymphoid cells. This epithelial layer can be quite thin and even lack a basement membrane in some regions."
>
> — Arambula et al. (2021)

Comparative histology confirms this reorganization: where crypt epithelium overlies lymphoid follicles it becomes lymphoepithelium, its stratified layers become minimal and indistinct, and it is infiltrated by lymphocytes, macrophages, plasma cells, dendritic cells and neutrophils (Raju et al., 2012):

> "The crypt epithelium where it overlaid or covered the regions of lymphoid follicles became lymphoepithelium (reticular epithelium). In these areas of lymphoepithelium, the different cell layers of stratified squamous epithelium were found to be minimal and got replaced by abundant lymphocytes and the layers were indistinct. An infiltration of macrophages, plasma cells, dendritic cells and neutrophils into the crypt epithelium was observed."
>
> — Raju et al. (2012)

The discontinuity of the basement membrane is directly attributable to heavy lymphocytic infiltration (Raju et al., 2012):

> "In the areas of lymphoepithelium the basement membrane was interrupted since lymphocytic infiltration was heavy into the epithelium."
>
> — Raju et al. (2012)

This reticulated crypt epithelium (lymphoepithelium) is emphasised as a key site of immune responses in the palatine tonsil (Raju et al., 2012):

> "The reticulated crypt epithelium, the lymphoepithelium (Perry & Whyte) plays a key role in immune responses in the palatine tonsil"
>
> — Raju et al. (2012)

At the M-cell level, mature M cells within this epithelium have characteristic ultrastructure — irregular sparse microvilli and a basolateral pocket harbouring lymphocytes (Kanaya et al., 2012):

> "PP M cells exhibit unique morphological characteristics including irregular and sparse microvilli, and a pocket-like invagination of the basolateral plasma membrane"
>
> — Kanaya et al. (2012)

Both surface and crypt epithelial cells build their cytoskeleton from keratins, whose expression pattern reflects epithelial type and differentiation state (Crowell et al., 2025):

> "Both surface and crypt epithelial cells contain keratins, a protein family that forms intermediate filaments and maintains cell structure and function, contributing to mechanical strength and protection from stress. Keratin expression profiles depends on the type of epithelium, the differentiation status of the cells, and the biological context"
>
> — Crowell et al. (2025)
