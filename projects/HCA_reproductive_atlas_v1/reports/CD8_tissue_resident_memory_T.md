# CD8+ Tissue-Resident Memory T Cells in the Female Reproductive Tract

Atlas: An integrated multimodal pan-organ atlas of the female reproductive system across the lifespan (DOI: 10.64898/2026.06.10.731198)
Scope: adult
Tissue context: female reproductive tract mucosa — predominantly fallopian tube and uterus/endometrium (also cervix, ovary, vagina)

## Summary

CD8+ tissue-resident memory T cells (CD8+ Trm; also "CD8+ TDRM" in modular nomenclature) are a non-circulating memory T cell population that lodges permanently in barrier tissues rather than recirculating through blood and lymph. In the Human Female Reproductive System Cell Atlas v1, this population (fine code `Immune_CD8_T_RM`, n=15,131 cells) is defined by expression of CD8A, CD8B, the tissue-retention integrins ITGA1 (CD49a) and ITGAE (CD103), CD101, and the chemokine receptor CXCR6, together with the absence of the circulating/egress markers KLF2, SELL, CCR7 and GZMK (Cohen et al., 2026). This marker profile mirrors the canonical human Trm core signature (Kumar et al., 2017) and reflects a well-characterised residency program in which downregulation of KLF2 and its target S1PR1 fixes cells in the tissue (Skon et al., 2013). Across the human female reproductive tract, CD8+ Trm are the dominant memory CD8 population of the endometrium, uterus and cervicovaginal mucosa, where they provide rapid local immune surveillance against sexually transmitted and other mucosal pathogens (Southcombe et al., 2017; Rodríguez-García et al., 2020; Lund et al., 2023). The atlas notes that a portion of this cluster expresses GZMB but not GZMK, consistent with the retained cytotoxic potential of mucosal CD8+ Trm (Cohen et al., 2026).

## Markers

The atlas assigns CD8+ Trm the positive markers CD8A, CD8B, ITGA1 (CD49a), ITGAE (CD103), CD101 and CXCR6, and defines the population against negative markers GZMK, KLF2, SELL (L-selectin) and CCR7, with a subset expressing GZMB but not GZMK (Cohen et al., 2026, Supplementary Table 2). This combination recapitulates the canonical human Trm signature. Kumar et al. (2017) simultaneously profiled exactly this panel of residency markers across human tissues:

> "Simultaneous expression of CD49a, CD103, CD101, CXCR6, CX3CR1, PD-1, and CD69 was visualized using t-SNE analysis."
>
> — Kumar et al. (2017)

**ITGAE (CD103) and ITGA1 (CD49a)** are adhesion integrins rather than passive biomarkers. CD103 (the αE integrin) binds E-cadherin on epithelial cells, while CD49a binds collagen (Topham & Reilly, 2018):

> "CD49a and CD103 are not merely biomarkers of TRM, they confer substrate specificities for cell adhesion to collagen and E-cadherin, respectively."
>
> — Topham & Reilly (2018)

In the reproductive tract specifically, the CD103–E-cadherin interaction anchors CD8+ Trm to the epithelium (Southcombe et al., 2017):

> "The ligand for CD103, E-cadherin, is highly expressed on endometrial glands and surface epithelium 31 , thus providing a mechanism for CD103+ CD8-T tissue residency, as is the case for the intestine 12 ."
>
> — Southcombe et al. (2017)

**CXCR6** is a residency-associated chemokine receptor consistently found on Trm across mucosal tissues (Wein et al., 2019):

> "Previous genomic analyses of tissue-resident memory CD8 T cells have shown that increased Cxcr6 expression is a common trait of T RM cells in many mucosal sites"
>
> — Wein et al. (2019)

Its role appears to be positioning and retention within the tissue rather than initial recruitment from the circulation (Wein et al., 2019), and in the ovary CXCR6 marks resident CD8+CD103+ tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes and supports their retention (Muthuswamy et al., 2020).

**CD101** and **CD49a** were among the markers found by the Lund group to be enriched on human female reproductive tract CD8 T cells relative to blood, alongside the canonical residency markers (Lund et al., 2023):

> "found, as expected, that putative T RM markers CD69 and CD103 were much more highly expressed by CD4 and CD8 T cells in the FRT tissues than in blood. Expression of CD49a, CD101, PD-1, and CCR5 was also higher."
>
> — Lund et al. (2023)

**Negative markers (KLF2, SELL, CCR7):** the loss of these genes is the mechanistic core of the residency program (see Function). KLF2 drives transcription of the egress receptors S1PR1 and CCR7, so its downregulation withdraws the tissue-exit signal (Kok et al., 2021; Walsh et al., 2019).

## Location

CD8+ Trm are, by definition, distributed across barrier tissues at sites of pathogen entry, including the female reproductive tract (Behr et al., 2019):

> "Their position in barrier tissues, including skin, intestine, female reproductive tract and lungs, places non-circulating CD8 + T RM cells at sites of pathogen entry, where they provide efficient early protection against local reinfection"
>
> — Behr et al. (2019)

### Endometrium / uterus

In the uterine mucosa, the great majority of memory CD8 T cells are resident. Southcombe et al. (2017) analysed CD103 and CD69 on endometrial CD8 T cells:

> "We hypothesised that this predominant CD8-T memory cell population would be Trm and therefore analysed expression of two key proteins that are directly involved with immune cell retention in tissues, CD103 and CD69. We found that the expression of both proteins was higher in the CD45RO+ versus CD45RO− population: CD103 (50% versus 20%) and CD69 (> 90% versus 60%)."
>
> — Southcombe et al. (2017)

The endometrium is a mucosal barrier that must balance defence with tolerance of implantation:

> "The mucosal surface of the uterus, the endometrium, is populated by innate and adaptive immune cells that provide a first line of defence towards pathogens."
>
> — Southcombe et al. (2017)

Independent uterine flow-cytometry data confirm that resident phenotype cells dominate the CD8 compartment (Kang et al., 2024):

> "Uterine CD4 + T cells and CD8 + T cells expressed obviously higher levels of CD69 and CD103 than that of blood."
>
> — Kang et al. (2024)

> "Gated on uterine CD44 + memory T cells, 61.3% CD4 + T and 77.4% CD8 + T cells in uterus were T RM cells"
>
> — Kang et al. (2024)

### Cervix, vagina and lower tract

Across the lower female reproductive tract, the CD69+CD103+ CD8 Trm fraction is the dominant CD8 population regardless of anatomical sub-site (Lund et al., 2023):

> "the CD69 + CD103+ fraction of CD8 T cells predominanted in the FRT regardless of tissue type (vagina, ectocervix, or endocervix)"
>
> — Lund et al. (2023)

> "CD8 + TRM (CD69 + CD103 + ) is the predominant fraction in CD8 + T cells of cervicovaginal tissue, while others are CD69 + single positive and double negative CD69 − CD103 − cells"
>
> — Dong et al. (2023)

In the female reproductive tract, CD103 essentially always co-occurs with the residency marker CD69 on CD8 T cells (Rodríguez-García et al., 2020):

> "we confirmed that in the FRT, almost all CD103+ CD8+ T cells coexpress CD69, while CD103− CD8+ T cells represent a mixture of CD69+ and CD69− cells"
>
> — Rodríguez-García et al. (2020)

CD8+ Trm are also present in the female genital tract of both healthy and HIV-positive women (Moylan et al., 2017), and the atlas is notable for resolving this tissue-resident landscape across all five reproductive organs, including the fallopian tube, at single-cell resolution (Cohen et al., 2026):

> "it defines the tissue-resident cellular landscape across reproductive organs at a resolution inaccessible to single-organ studies"
>
> — Cohen et al. (2026)

## Function

### Residency program (retention, not egress)

The defining functional feature of CD8+ Trm is a transcriptional switch away from recirculation. Skon et al. (2013) established the mechanism:

> "We found that CD8+ TRM cells lacked expression of the transcription factor KLF2 and its target gene S1pr1 (which encodes S1P1, a receptor for sphingosine 1-phosphate). Forced expression of S1P1 prevented the establishment of TRM cells."
>
> — Skon et al. (2013)

> "Hence, regulation of KLF2 and S1P1 provides a switch that dictates whether CD8+ T cells commit to recirculating or tissue-resident memory populations."
>
> — Skon et al. (2013)

Because KLF2 drives both S1PR1 and CCR7, losing KLF2 simultaneously removes the two receptors that would otherwise pull the cell back into circulation (Kok et al., 2021):

> "For instance, T RM cells show reduced expression of the cell surface molecules S1PR1 and CCR7 that promote T cells to leave NLTs, an observation that is explained by a lowered expression of the transcription factor KLF2, which drives S1PR1 and CCR7 transcription"
>
> — Kok et al. (2021)

This directly explains the atlas's negative markers KLF2, SELL and CCR7 for the `Immune_CD8_T_RM` cluster (Cohen et al., 2026). At the protein level, CD69 reinforces this by antagonising S1PR1 (Walsh et al., 2019):

> "The transcription factor KLF2 promotes S1PR1 expression, and both S1PR1 and KLF2 are downregulated in CD8 + T RM"
>
> — Walsh et al. (2019)

### Local immune surveillance

CD8+ Trm compensate for the decline of circulating T cell surveillance at barrier surfaces, providing durable frontline defence (Gebhardt & Mackay, 2012):

> "recent evidence suggests that a specialized subset of CD103+ memory T cells can permanently lodge and persist in peripheral tissues, and that these cells can compensate for the loss of peripheral immune surveillance by circulating memory T cells"
>
> — Gebhardt & Mackay (2012)

In the female reproductive tract this translates into rapid protection against recurrent mucosal infection (Rodríguez-García et al., 2020):

> "Long lasting residency in tissues coupled with their presence at mucosal sites, enables TRMs to provide rapid protection in response to secondary infections (10,11)."
>
> — Rodríguez-García et al. (2020)

A well-documented example is protection against genital herpes: pre-existing vaginal HSV-2-specific Trm restrict viral replication and spread (Chou & Li, 2018):

> "a report showed that pre-existing herpes simplex virus (HSV) 2 antigen-specific T RM cells at the vaginal mucosa protect hosts from lethal HSV-2 challenge by restricting viral replication at the site of infection as well as preventing the spread of virus to the peripheral nervous system"
>
> — Chou & Li (2018)

### Effector functions: cytotoxicity and cytokine-driven alarm

CD8+ Trm retain effector capacity. Upon antigen re-encounter they release pro-inflammatory cytokines that recruit other immune cells (Behr et al., 2019):

> "Upon antigen encounter, CD8 + T RM cells rapidly release pro-inflammatory cytokines, including interferon-γ (IFN-γ), which induce a tissuewide state of alert and initiate the recruitment of circulating B cells and memory T cells"
>
> — Behr et al. (2019)

They can also directly kill target cells (Behr et al., 2019):

> "In some tissues, CD8 + T RM cells can also directly lyse target cells and limit pathogen spread by employing cytotoxic mechanisms"
>
> — Behr et al. (2019)

This cytotoxicity is granzyme-mediated (Chou & Li, 2018):

> "As CD8 + T cells can directly lyse infected target cells through the release of granzymes and perforin, several studies reported granzyme B expression in T RM cells as well"
>
> — Chou & Li (2018)

The granzyme B (GZMB) signal is directly relevant to the atlas annotation, which records that a portion of the `Immune_CD8_T_RM` cluster expresses GZMB but not GZMK (Cohen et al., 2026). Consistent with this, memory CD8 T cells in healthy human cervicovaginal tissue are predominantly granzyme B positive (Davé et al., 2019):

> "We examined the T cell compartment in healthy human cervicovaginal tissue (CVT) and found that most CD8 T cells were granzyme B+ and TCF-1-."
>
> — Davé et al. (2019)

Cytokine release also amplifies local immunity by recruiting further effectors (Dong et al., 2023):

> "Once TRM recognises the cognate peptide, it releases cytokines IL-2, TNFa, and IFNg, thereby upregulating adhesion molecules and chemokines and promoting the recruitment of circulating memory T cells and B cells into tissues"
>
> — Dong et al. (2023)

## Structure / Morphology

No dedicated ultrastructural or morphological description of this population was found in the traversed literature. The relevant "structural" features are the adhesion integrins that physically anchor the cells: CD103 (ITGAE) binding epithelial E-cadherin and CD49a (ITGA1) binding collagen (Topham & Reilly, 2018; Southcombe et al., 2017), positioning CD8+ Trm within epithelial compartments of the reproductive mucosa.
