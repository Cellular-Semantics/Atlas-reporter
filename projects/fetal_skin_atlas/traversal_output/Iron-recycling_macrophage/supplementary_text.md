Referee #2 (Remarks to the Author): Gopee et al. present a study on prenatal human skin, focusing on macrophages and their potential role in angiogenesis. The team has created a comprehensive atlas of prenatal human skin using various data sources, including single-cell and spatial transcriptomics. Their findings suggest that interactions between immune and non-immune cells are crucial for proper skin development. To validate the findings, a skin organoid model was compared to prenatal and adult skin. Additionally, the research employs the human prenatal skin cell atlas to gain insights into the pathogenesis of genetic hair and skin disorders. The data from the skin atlas indicated that macrophages and the growth factors they produce play a significant role in prenatal endothelial cell development. As proof of principle, a co-culture model and the organoid model were taken to show that macrophages indeed enhance angiogenesis. In summary, the study strengthens the long- standing fact (mainly observed in mice) that particularly macrophages have additional roles in tissue development beyond their traditional immune functions. The study is thoroughly done and will be a fantastic resource for many fields (e.g. developmental biology, macrophage biology). The bioinformatics analysis is easy to follow, despite its complexity.
