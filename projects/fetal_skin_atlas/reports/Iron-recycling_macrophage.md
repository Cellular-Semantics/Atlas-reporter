# Iron-Recycling Macrophages in Prenatal Human Skin

Atlas: A prenatal skin atlas reveals immune regulation of human skin morphogenesis (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-024-08002-x)
Scope: fetal
Cell Ontology: [tissue-resident macrophage](http://purl.obolibrary.org/obo/CL_0000864) (CL:0000864, broad match — no exact CL term)

## Summary

Iron-recycling macrophages are one of four macrophage subsets identified in prenatal human skin (7–17 post-conception weeks; PCW) by Gopee et al. (2024). They are defined by expression of CD5L, APOE, VCAM1, TIMD4, and SLC40A1, markers shared with erythrophagocytic macrophages of the spleen and liver in adult tissues (Domínguez Conde et al., 2022). In the prenatal skin, iron-recycling macrophages are spatially enriched in the myeloid/megakaryocyte-erythroid microenvironment (ME2) alongside LYVE1+ macrophages. Their primary described function in this context is promotion of endothelial cell chemotaxis, contributing to the broader role of skin macrophages in supporting prenatal angiogenesis (Gopee et al., 2024). The subset was first characterised across multiple developing human organs by Suo et al. (2022), who placed it within a broader taxonomy of eight prenatal macrophage groupings.

## Markers

Iron-recycling macrophages in prenatal skin are annotated by Gopee et al. (2024) on the basis of five marker genes — CD5L, APOE, VCAM1, TIMD4, and SLC40A1 — as identified by differential expression analysis (two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum test, Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted; Supplementary Table 22) and gene set enrichment analysis (Supplementary Table 24). These markers were first reported for this macrophage grouping in a multi-organ developmental immune atlas (Suo et al., 2022) and were subsequently used to annotate the subset in prenatal skin.

The cross-tissue significance of the marker profile is underscored by Domínguez Conde et al. (2022), who showed that erythrophagocytic macrophages in adult spleen and liver — the canonical iron-recycling population —

> "shared high expression of CD5L, SCL40A1 and the transcription factor SPIC"

> — Domínguez Conde et al. (2022)

and that a small population with this signature also appeared in lymph nodes. The presence of SLC40A1 (ferroportin), the sole known iron exporter in mammalian cells, is particularly informative: its expression marks cells specialised for cellular iron efflux following erythrophagocytosis. TIMD4 is a marker associated with long-lived tissue-resident macrophages seeded by fetal progenitors (Mass et al., 2023), while CD5L and APOE are broadly expressed by tissue-homeostatic macrophage states.

In the prenatal multi-organ atlas, Suo et al. (2022) identified iron-recycling macrophages as one of eight broad macrophage groupings — alongside LYVE1hi, MHC class IIhi, Kupffer-like, microglia-like TREM2hi, osteoclast, and proliferating macrophages. These groupings were defined by transcriptome profile across multiple developing organs:

> "The myeloid compartment captured the development from committed myeloid progenitors to neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, and DCs ( fig. S4, G and H). Our cross-tissue analysis distinguished three distinct subsets of monocytes, characterized by a differential distribution between prenatal bone marrow and peripheral tissues and by the expression of CXCR4, CCR2, or IL1B (17). Among macrophages, we identified eight broad macrophage groupings based on their transcriptome profile ( fig. S4H)"

> — Suo et al. (2022)

## Location

### In prenatal skin

Iron-recycling macrophages are assigned to microenvironment ME2 ('Myeloid/Megakaryocyte-erythroid enriched') in prenatal skin, based on non-negative matrix factorisation of cell2location spatial abundance profiles (Gopee et al., 2024; Supplementary Table 40). LYVE1+ macrophages share this spatial niche. By contrast, TML macrophages are enriched in ME1 and MHC-II+ macrophages in ME5, which co-localise more closely with endothelial and neural cells. The ME2 assignment places iron-recycling macrophages in a region enriched for erythroid and megakaryocyte progenitors, consistent with the subset's functional specialisation in iron metabolism.

### Across developing organs

In the multi-organ developmental immune atlas, Suo et al. (2022) found that iron-recycling macrophage neighborhoods were enriched at later stages of gestation:

> "a large fraction of neighborhoods within the ironrecycling and MHCII hi macrophage populations were enriched in later stages of gestation. We found that these subpopulations upregulated genes encoding for immune effector functions ( Fig. 2B; fig. S14C; and table S1)."

> — Suo et al. (2022)

This contrasts with LYVE1hi and proliferating macrophages, which were enriched in early gestation. The adult erythrophagocytic counterparts are primarily tissue-restricted to spleen and liver (Domínguez Conde et al., 2022), making the appearance of this population in developing skin a notable feature of the prenatal immune landscape.

## Function

### Endothelial cell chemotaxis and angiogenesis

The principal function attributed to iron-recycling macrophages in prenatal skin is promotion of endothelial cell chemotaxis. Gene module expression analysis in Gopee et al. (2024) showed:

> "Gene ontology analysis showed that the four macrophage subsets (LYVE1 + , MHCII + , TML and iron-recycling) expressed gene programs that drive angiogenesis (Supplementary Tables 23-26). Gene module expression profiles suggested that sprouting angiogenesis (growth of new vessels) was promoted by LYVE1 + and TML macrophages, blood vessel morphogenesis by LYVE1 + macrophages and endothelial cell chemotaxis by iron-recycling macrophages (Extended Data Fig. 8i and Supplementary Table 27). Consistent with this finding, multiplex RNAscope and immunofluorescence staining showed LYVE1 + and TML macrophages in close proximity to endothelial cells (Fig. 4a and Supplementary Video 1). Predicted ligandreceptor interactions were consistent with reciprocal communication between macrophages and endothelial cells to support angiogenesis, chemotaxis and cell migration (for example, CXCL8-ACKR1 and CCL8-ACKR1) 10,70 (Extended Data Fig. 9a and Supplementary Table 28)."

> — Gopee et al. (2024)

The importance of macrophages for prenatal skin vascular development is further illustrated by comparison with a skin organoid (SkO) model that lacks immune cells:

> "Our data suggested that macrophages contribute to prenatal skin angiogenesis. Consistent with this hypothesis, we observed fewer and less heterogeneous endothelial cells in the immunodeficient SkOs compared to prenatal skin, despite the formation of well-developed HFs, epidermis and neural cells (Fig. 4b,c, Extended Data Fig. 9b and Supplementary Table 29)."

> — Gopee et al. (2024)

The pro-angiogenic role of macrophages in this context extends to regulation of VEGF receptor expression on endothelial cells:

> "Anti-angiogenic genes (for example, WNT5A) and corresponding receptors were highly expressed in SkO, whereas pro-angiogenic genes (for example, CXCL8) were upregulated in prenatal skin and primarily expressed by macrophages (Extended Data Fig. 11a and Supplementary Tables 30-34). Although expression of vascular endothelial growth factors (VEGF), VEGFA and VEGFB were increased in SkO cells, their receptors (KDR and FLT1) on SkO capillary arterioles were downregulated compared to prenatal skin (Extended Data Fig. 11b,c). These receptors are known downstream targets of GATA2, which has a key role in angiogenesis during development and regulates VEGF-induced endothelial cell migration and sprouting in vitro 72 . Regulon analysis showed that GATA2 and related regulons (for example, NFATC1) were downregulated in SkO capillary arterioles (Extended Data Fig. 11d,e). Several target genes of GATA2 and NFATC1 (for example, VWF), which were expressed across the venular trajectory pseudotime and are involved in endothelial cell differentiation 73 , were downregulated in the SkO capillary arterioles compared to prenatal skin (Extended Data Fig. 11c,f). An orthogonal approach (NicheNet 74 ) identified macrophage-expressed VEGFA as one of the top upstream ligands that regulate differences in GATA2 expression between prenatal skin and SkO endothelial cells (Extended Data Fig. 11g and Supplementary Tables 35-37). These findings suggest that high VEGF production in the SkO cannot compensate for missing macrophage-related factors that drive GATA2 activity and downstream VEGF receptor expression (Fig. 4e)."

> — Gopee et al. (2024)

### Broader developmental context

Suo et al. (2022) noted that early macrophages across developing organs may contribute to tissue morphogenesis before assuming classical immune roles:

> "These findings suggest that early macrophages and mast cells may contribute to angiogenesis, tissue morphogenesis and homeostasis as previously reported (28)(29)(30), prior to adopting traditional immunological functions. Notably, acquisition of macrophage antigenpresentation properties (e.g., MHCII upregulation) between 10 and 12 pcw coincided with the expansion of adaptive lymphocytes (fig. S1E) and development of lymphatic vessels and lymph nodes (31)."

> — Suo et al. (2022)

The later-gestation enrichment of iron-recycling macrophage neighborhoods — and their upregulation of immune effector genes — is consistent with a transition from morphogenetic to immunological functions as gestation progresses.
